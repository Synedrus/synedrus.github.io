Untangle systems that have become too complex to change safely or confidently.

Get in touch

Architecture tends to become complicated one reasonable decision at a time.

A workaround becomes permanent, because “there’s no more permanent solution than a temporary one”. Another integration gets bolted on. Responsibilities blur between components. Teams become reluctant to touch particular parts of the system because nobody is entirely sure what else will break.

Eventually, complexity stops being an architectural concern and starts becoming a delivery problem.

Architecture Simplification is for engineering teams whose systems have become unnecessarily difficult to change, understand or operate safely.

Find where complexity is costing you

We work with the team to understand where architectural complexity is creating real friction.

The target is not complexity for its own sake. A complicated system may be completely justified.

The problem is complexity that is contributing to things such as:

  • slow or risky changes;
  • recurring delivery problems;
  • unclear ownership or system boundaries;
  • fragile dependencies;
  • excessive operational overhead;
  • difficult diagnosis when something fails; or
  • engineering effort being consumed simply keeping the existing structure together.

The objective is to identify which parts of the architecture are actively working against the team.

Simplify with a reason

We help define and, where appropriate, implement a more workable technical direction.

That does not automatically mean replacing everything, moving platforms, introducing a fashionable architecture, or beginning a transformation programme. Sometimes the right answer is to remove something, separate responsibilities more clearly, replace a fragile path while leaving everything around it alone, and sometimes the existing architecture is fundamentally sound and the real problem lies elsewhere.

The intervention follows the problem rather than forcing the problem to fit a preferred technology.

The outcome

The goal is an engineering environment that is easier to reason about and safer to change.

You should come away with:

  • a clearer understanding of where architectural complexity is hurting the business;
  • a practical direction for reducing that complexity;
  • technical decisions tied to real delivery or operational consequences; and
  • less friction between what the business needs to change and what the systems will safely allow.

Architecture Simplification is about removing technical structure that has stopped earning its keep.
